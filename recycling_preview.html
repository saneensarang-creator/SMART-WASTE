Waste Management Facts

  1. Low global recycling rates: Only a small fraction of many materials (especially plastics) is actually recycled worldwide — reducing and reusing are critical first steps.
  2. Paper saves trees: Recycling one ton of paper can save about 17 trees and significant water and energy.
  3. Composting matters: Organic waste in landfills produces methane; composting diverts organics and reduces greenhouse gases.
  4. Electronics contain value: E-waste holds recoverable metals (gold, copper) — proper recycling prevents pollution and recovers resources.
  5. Contamination reduces reuse: Food and non-recyclable items in recycling bins can spoil whole batches — rinse and sort when possible.
  6. Hazardous items need care: Batteries, paint, and chemicals must be disposed of properly to avoid soil and water pollution.
  7. Waste-to-energy tradeoffs: Incineration can recover energy but should complement — not replace — prevention, reuse, and recycling.
  8. Circular economy benefits: Designing products for reuse and repair keeps materials in use longer and reduces extraction of virgin resources.
